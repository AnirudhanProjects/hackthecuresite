Students should get a real shot at these problems.

Most school science stops at the poster. We wanted something closer to the real thing: a documented problem, mentors who work in the field, and a week to build. Anyone aged 13 to 18 can enter, from anywhere, for nothing.

  1. Real problems, not made-up prompts

    Every track comes from a documented gap in global health. The brief tells you who is affected and what has already been tried, so you never start from a blank page.

  2. Mentors who stay all week

    Two mentors are assigned to each track and stay in its breakout room for the whole week. They hold office hours every day. You get feedback while you can still act on it, and there is nothing to book.

  3. Free, and open to anyone

    There is no entry fee and no application to pass. You need a laptop, an internet connection and a week over winter break. Hack The Cure is run as a non-profit by volunteers.

Pick the problem you want to spend the week on.

You choose one track when you sign up. Each has its own breakout room, its own two mentors and its own prizes, so a nutrition project never gets judged against a diagnostics project.

How the week runs.

Two ceremonies and six days of building. Mentors are in their track rooms for all of it.

  1. 1

    Day 1 · Sun 27 Dec

    Opening ceremony and track kickoff

    We go through each subject brief and hand out the judging rubric. If you turn up without a team, team forming runs all day!

  2. 2–7

    Days 2–7 · Mon 28 Dec – Sat 2 Jan

    Build time, with mentors on hand every day

    Both mentors for your track hold office hours in its room every day. Drop in when you get stuck. Nothing to book, no waiting for a review slot, just send a Discord message!

    Office hours: times announced closer to the event.

  3. 8

    Day 8 · Sun 3 Jan

    Submissions close, judging and closing

    Projects and demo videos are due. Judging happens within each track via Zoom. Winners are announced at the closing ceremony, and every team gets its written scores afterwards.

The rubric is published before you start.

Two judges score every project out of 100 using the weights below. You get the same rubric on day one. Nothing is scored that is not on this list.
